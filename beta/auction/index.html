How the auction works

A regular auction: bidding starts at your starting price and goes up while bidders outbid each other.

  1. Bidding opens at your starting price. That's also the least your item can sell for.
  2. Bidders outbid each other, higher and higher. Every new bid has to beat the one before.
  3. When the bidding stops, the highest bid wins. Going once, going twice… sold!
  4. Nobody bids your starting price? Then it doesn't sell and you get it back.

When your item sells, you receive the sale price minus our cut. A starting price a little under the item's value gets the bidding going; just don't set it lower than you'd be happy to sell for.

How the auction works

We use the Dutch clock, the way flowers are sold at the big Dutch flower auctions: the price doesn't go up while people outbid each other, it starts high and keeps dropping until someone takes it.

  1. The clock starts at your starting bid. Every item gets its turn on the clock.
  2. The price ticks down, lower and lower. Bidders watch it fall and wait for a price they like.
  3. The first bidder to claim it wins it at that price. Wait too long and someone else grabs it first: there's no second chance once it's claimed.
  4. The clock never goes below your lowest limit. If nobody claims it by then, it doesn't sell and you get it back.

When your item sells, you receive the sale price minus our cut. Set your starting bid high enough to leave room to drop, and your lowest limit at the least you'd be happy to sell for.

Example: starting bid 49 DB, lowest limit 9 DB, claimed at 31 DB
